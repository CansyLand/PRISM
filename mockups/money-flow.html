A 10× long on $100,000 notional ($10,000 margin). Perps are ~zero-sum: the winner's P&L is paid by the other side. PRISM and 1Click just skim fees. Tab ③ shows how the fill itself happens — the intent auction across competing solvers.

There is no limit-order book. The trader's order is an intent: "buy $100k of wNEAR at 10×, best price." 1Click fans that intent out to competing solvers (market makers who already hold the liquidity). Each solver returns a price + fee; the best all-in price wins and executes. That winning fill is the other side of the trade — and the P&L of the position is absorbed by whoever is net short (the vault/shorts, tabs ①/②).

Tokens: the winner sends the trader wNEAR and receives the trader's USDC (the swap the intent describes). 1Click takes a small cut of the solver's fee for routing. The whole auction ran in ~380ms, off-chain; only the final fill is settled on NEAR.
